Video Plays but No Sound on Android? How to Fix AC3, E-AC3 and DTS Audio

By Vidnexa Team·5 min read·Updated November 2026
Quick answer: Your current player most likely can't decode the file's audio codec, usually AC3, E-AC3 or DTS. Open the video in Vidnexa Player, which plays AC3, E-AC3 and DTS audio, or switch to another audio track from the playback menu. Also rule out the simple things: media volume, mute and a connected Bluetooth device.

The picture plays fine, but there's silence. Before you blame the file or your speaker, check the audio format: movie files often carry Dolby Digital (AC3), Dolby Digital Plus (E-AC3) or DTS audio, and not every phone or player can decode them.

Why does my video have no sound on Android?

A video file holds a video stream and one or more audio streams, each compressed with a codec. If your player understands the video codec but not the audio codec, you get exactly this symptom: picture, no sound. Movie downloads and Blu-ray or web rips often use surround-sound codecs such as AC3, E-AC3 or DTS. Some phones and stock players don't include decoders for these, so the audio is silently skipped.

What are AC3 and E-AC3?

AC3 is Dolby Digital, the classic 5.1 surround format used on DVDs and TV broadcasts. E-AC3 (Dolby Digital Plus) is its newer, more efficient version, common in streaming-service downloads and web rips. DTS is a competing surround format found on many Blu-ray rips. They're all perfectly normal formats; the problem is only whether your player can decode them.

Fix 1: Open the video in a player that plays AC3, E-AC3 and DTS

The quickest fix is a player that decodes these formats itself instead of relying on your phone. Vidnexa Player plays AC3 (Dolby Digital), E-AC3 (Dolby Digital Plus) and DTS audio, so a movie that's silent in your gallery app plays with sound. Install it, open the same file, and the audio should start straight away.

Fix 2: Switch the audio track

Many movie files include two or more audio tracks, for example an E-AC3 5.1 track and an AAC stereo track, or several languages. If the track that's selected has no sound, open the playback menu and pick a different one. In Vidnexa Player, open Player Controls › Audio › Audio track to switch between the languages and tracks in dual-audio files. Not sure which codec a track uses? Media info, under Save & Share, is the place to look.

Fix 3: Check volume, mute and Bluetooth

  • Press the volume-up key while the video plays, so you change the media volume, not the ringer.
  • Check that the player itself isn't muted (in Vidnexa: Player Controls › Audio › Mute).
  • Sound there but very quiet? Check your phone's volume and the Boost option in Vidnexa's Player Controls.
  • If earbuds or a speaker are connected, the sound may be going there. Turn Bluetooth off and test again.

Fix 4: Sound plays, but out of sync

If the sound is there but ahead of or behind the picture, it's a sync issue, not a codec issue. Bluetooth headphones add delay. In Vidnexa Player, open Player Controls › Audio › Audio sync to move the sound earlier or later until it matches.

Fix 5: Convert the audio only as a last resort

If no player on your phone can play the audio, you can re-encode just the audio track to AAC on a computer, keeping the video untouched. This takes time, so try the fixes above first.

Frequently asked questions

Why does my video have picture but no sound?
The player supports the video codec but not the audio codec. Movie files often use AC3, E-AC3 or DTS audio. Open the file in a player that decodes these, such as Vidnexa Player, or switch to another audio track.

What is E-AC3 audio?
E-AC3, also called Dolby Digital Plus, is a surround-sound audio format used in many streaming downloads and web rips. Not every phone or player can decode it.
