Where WhatsApp keeps statuses on your phone

When you open someone's status, WhatsApp downloads it to a hidden folder on your phone so it can play. That's the folder a status saver reads. Because the folder is temporary, the file is cleared automatically once the status expires, which is 24 hours after it was posted.

On newer versions of Android, apps are limited in which folders they can read, so you may be asked to allow access once before saved statuses appear.

If a status isn't showing up

  • Open it in WhatsApp first. A status you haven't viewed hasn't been stored on your phone yet.
  • Give it a few seconds, then go back and open the Status Saver again.
  • Check the 24-hour limit. If the status has expired, it can't be saved any more.
  • Check permissions. If you denied folder access earlier, allow it from the prompt or your phone's settings.

Save and share responsibly

A status is someone else's personal content. Keeping a copy for yourself is one thing, and posting it elsewhere is another. A few ground rules:

  • Only save what the owner would be comfortable with.
  • Ask before you share someone's photo or video outside WhatsApp.
  • Never use saved content to embarrass, impersonate or harass anyone.
  • Remember that music and videos inside a status may be protected by copyright.

Keeping your own statuses

Where are WhatsApp statuses stored? (.Statuses folder)

When you view a status, WhatsApp saves a temporary copy in a hidden folder called .Statuses. The dot at the start of the name is what hides it from most gallery and file apps. Where it lives depends on your Android version:

  • Android 11 and newer: Internal storage › Android › media › com.whatsapp › WhatsApp › Media › .Statuses
  • Android 10 and older: Internal storage › WhatsApp › Media › .Statuses
  • WhatsApp Business uses its own folder: Android › media › com.whatsapp.w4b › WhatsApp Business › Media › .Statuses

If you open the folder in a file manager, you may need to turn on Show hidden files first. Files in it are cleared automatically once a status expires, which is why copying them to your gallery matters.

What changed on Android 11 and above?

Android 11 introduced scoped storage, which limits which folders apps can read. WhatsApp moved its media into the protected Android › media area at the same time. As a result, a status saver can no longer read statuses on its own: you have to grant access to the folder once, through Android's own folder-permission screen. If Vidnexa asks for access the first time you open Status Saver, allow it, and your viewed statuses will appear. Some older file managers can't open this folder at all on Android 11+, which is another reason a built-in saver is easier.
