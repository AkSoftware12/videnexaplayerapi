Where MX Player still wins

MX Player's biggest strength is simply time and scale. Over a decade of updates means its hardware-acceleration tuning and codec edge-case handling are extremely mature — it has likely been tested against more obscure, oddly-encoded files than almost any other Android player. With 1B+ installs, its gesture controls (pinch to zoom, swipe for brightness/volume) set the pattern most other video players, including Vidnexa, still follow today.

Where Vidnexa Player differs

Vidnexa was built more recently, around how people watch now — which shows up in a few specific places MX Player doesn't cover:

  • HDR+ picture enhancement — Vidnexa's HDR+ mode boosts contrast, brightness and colour on any video, with presets such as Warm, Neon and Sepia. MX Player plays HDR files, but a picture-enhancement mode like this isn't part of what it's known for.
  • Built-in screen casting — both apps support Google Cast, so either can send video to a Chromecast or a TV with Chromecast built in on the same Wi-Fi network.
  • AI voice control — Vidnexa can play, pause, skip and adjust volume on spoken commands. MX Player's gesture controls are touch-only.
  • One app, more jobs — Vidnexa also includes a music player, a full photo gallery with a duplicate/junk cleaner and 4K photo enhancement, and a file manager — MX Player stays focused on video.
  • Private vault — Vidnexa has a PIN/biometric-locked private folder for files you don't want showing up in your gallery. MX Player's "Kids Lock" restricts leaving the app during playback, which solves a different problem.

Ads and pricing

Neither app is ad-free by default. MX Player's free version shows ads, with an ad-free experience available through its paid Pro tier. Vidnexa Player's free version also shows ads, with an optional paid tier to remove them. If ads are your main concern, that choice comes down to the paid tier either way — not which free app you start with.

Our take

If you mainly need a reliable player for an unusual or old file and nothing else, MX Player's decade of codec refinement is hard to argue with. If you like a punchier picture, cast to a TV regularly, want hands-free control, or would rather not juggle four separate apps for video, music, gallery and files — Vidnexa covers more of that in one install.

Is Vidnexa Player better than MX Player?
It depends what you need. MX Player has a huge user base and mature hardware-acceleration tuning. Vidnexa adds HDR+ picture enhancement, AI voice control, built-in screen casting and a private vault — all in one app without installing separate tools.

Does MX Player have ads?
Yes, the free version of MX Player shows ads; an ad-free experience requires its paid Pro tier. Vidnexa Player's free version also shows ads, with an optional paid tier to remove them.

Can MX Player cast to a TV like Vidnexa?
Yes. Both MX Player and Vidnexa Player support Google Cast, so both can send video to a Chromecast or a TV with Chromecast built in. TVs without Google Cast, such as most Samsung and LG models, need your phone's screen mirroring instead.

MX Player focuses on broad codec and format support rather than picture enhancement. Vidnexa Player includes an HDR+ mode that boosts contrast, brightness and colour, with several presets.

Which app supports more video formats?
Both support a very wide range. MX Player has been refining format support for over a decade. Vidnexa Player supports 200+ video and audio formats including MKV, HEVC, AVI and more, with no separate codec packs.
